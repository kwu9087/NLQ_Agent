## `asking_vs_paid`: pass

**Q:** How does the average asking price compare with the average price paid, by category?

*Tests:* Every sale is at its asking price: a gap means pricier listings sold more, not that buyers paid above asking.

**Answer:**

> In every category, the average price paid per ticket is higher than the average asking price, by roughly $18 to $25. Opera has the biggest gap ($326.24 paid vs. $301.15 asked, a $25.09 difference), followed by Plays ($325.15 vs. $300.69). Musicals have the smallest gap, at $18.48 ($316.77 vs. $298.29), and the full breakdown is in the table.

**Assumptions:**

- Covers all listings and sales in the data (January 7, 2025 to January 7, 2026).
- Both averages are per ticket. The asking average gives each listing weight by how many tickets it offered, and the paid average gives each sale weight by how many tickets it included.
- Every sale happens at exactly the listing's asking price, so a gap between the two averages doesn't mean buyers paid more or less than asked. A higher price paid means the pricier listings sold more of their tickets, and a lower price paid means the cheaper listings sold better.
- Only categories that have events are shown (Pop, Plays, Musicals, Opera and NBA). The other categories have no listings.

```sql
WITH asking AS (SELECT category, category_group, SUM(1.0 * asking_price_per_ticket * tickets_listed) AS asking_value, SUM(tickets_listed) AS tickets_listed FROM listing_detail GROUP BY category, category_group), paid AS (SELECT category, SUM(1.0 * sale_total) AS amount_paid, SUM(tickets) AS tickets_sold FROM sales_detail GROUP BY category) SELECT a.category, a.category_group, ROUND(a.asking_value / a.tickets_listed, 2) AS avg_asking_price, ROUND(p.amount_paid / p.tickets_sold, 2) AS avg_price_paid, ROUND(p.amount_paid / p.tickets_sold - a.asking_value / a.tickets_listed, 2) AS paid_minus_asking, a.tickets_listed, p.tickets_sold FROM asking a LEFT JOIN paid p ON p.category = a.category ORDER BY avg_asking_price DESC
```
